Private label vs. flagship brand: share by retailer

Python · pandas · GeoPandas · matplotlib  |  Download the notebook (.ipynb)

A manufacturer sells specialty inputs (adjuvants, micronutrients, biologicals) under its flagship brand. Many of the same products also go out as private label, the retailer's own brand. The question: for products available both ways, how much of each retailer's volume is private label, and where is that share concentrated?

The tricky part is that the two versions don't share a product ID. Equivalents have to be paired through naming conventions in the product hierarchy.

About the data. The original pulled five crop years of shipment lines from a sales warehouse. Product names, retailers, and numbers here are all simulated with generic names. The pairing logic, unit handling, and share calculations follow the original.

1. Simulated shipment lines

Each line has a retailer, an item description, a product-line code (line_code), a unit of measure, quantity, and revenue. Private-label lines start with PL , flagship lines with FB . Some products only exist under the flagship brand. A few ship in quarts instead of gallons, and some retailers carry no private label at all.

rng = np.random.default_rng(5)
PRODUCTS = [  # (common name, category, has private-label twin, $/gal)
    ("SURFACTANT 90", "ADJUVANT", True, 22), ("CROP OIL CONC", "ADJUVANT", True, 14),
    ("DRIFT CONTROL", "ADJUVANT", True, 38), ("WATER CONDITIONER", "ADJUVANT", True, 11),
    ("DEPOSITION AID", "ADJUVANT", True, 45), ("HS METHYLATED OIL", "ADJUVANT", True, 19),
    ("ZINC 9%", "MICRONUTRIENT", True, 28), ("BORON 10%", "MICRONUTRIENT", True, 24),
    ("MANGANESE 5%", "MICRONUTRIENT", False, 31), ("FOLIAR NPK", "MICRONUTRIENT", False, 26),
    ("BIO SEED TRT", "BIOLOGICAL", False, 120), ("PGR PLUS", "PLANT GROWTH REGULATOR", False, 85),
]
# commodity adjuvants lean private label; premium products much less so
PL_FACTOR = {"SURFACTANT 90": 1.6, "CROP OIL CONC": 1.7, "WATER CONDITIONER": 1.8, "HS METHYLATED OIL": 1.2,
             "DRIFT CONTROL": 0.6, "DEPOSITION AID": 0.35, "ZINC 9%": 1.0, "BORON 10%": 0.9}
TOWNS = ["Cedar Valley", "Prairie Creek", "River Bend", "Twin Lakes", "North Star", "Heartland", "Golden Plains",
         "Maple Ridge", "Big Sioux", "Lone Tree", "Clear Fork", "Elk Horn", "Willow Run", "Stony Point", "Pine Bluff",
         "Red Cedar", "Sand Hills", "Three Rivers", "Bluestem", "Kettle Creek"]
SUFFIX = ["Ag Co-op", "Farm Supply", "Agronomy", "Farmers Union"]

shape = states.union_all()
pts = []
while len(pts) < 70:
    p = gpd.points_from_xy([rng.uniform(-104, -81)], [rng.uniform(36.5, 49)])[0]
    if shape.contains(p):
        pts.append(p)
retailers = gpd.GeoDataFrame({"retailer_id": [f"R{i:03d}" for i in range(70)],
                              "retailer": rng.permutation([f"{t} {s}" for t in TOWNS for s in SUFFIX])[:70]},
                             geometry=pts, crs=4326).sjoin(states[["STUSPS", "geometry"]]).rename(columns={"STUSPS": "state"})
retailers["lat"], retailers["lon"] = retailers.geometry.y, retailers.geometry.x
# private-label appetite: higher in the west, plus noise; ~20% carry none
retailers["pl_appetite"] = np.clip(0.15 + 0.025 * (-retailers.lon - 88) + rng.normal(0, 0.12, 70), 0.02, 0.85)
retailers.loc[rng.random(70) < 0.2, "pl_appetite"] = 0
retailers["size"] = rng.lognormal(0, 0.8, 70)

rows = []
for _, r in retailers.iterrows():
    for name, cat, has_pl, price in PRODUCTS:
        if rng.random() < 0.25:
            continue
        for year in range(2021, 2026):
            base_gal = r["size"] * rng.lognormal(6.5, 0.5) * (1 + 0.06 * (year - 2021))
            pl_share = min(0.95, r.pl_appetite * PL_FACTOR[name] * (1 + 0.12 * (year - 2021))) if has_pl else 0
            for brand, share in [("FB", 1 - pl_share), ("PL", pl_share)]:
                gal = base_gal * share
                if gal < 1:
                    continue
                uom, qty = ("QT", gal * 4) if name in ("ZINC 9%", "BORON 10%") and rng.random() < 0.5 else ("GA", gal)
                unit_price = price * (0.82 if brand == "PL" else 1) * rng.uniform(0.95, 1.05)
                rows.append({"retailer_id": r.retailer_id, "crop_year": year, "category": cat,
                             "item_desc": f"{brand} {name} {'2.5GA' if uom == 'GA' else '1QT'}",
                             "line_code": f"{brand} {name}", "uom": uom, "qty": round(qty, 1),
                             "revenue": round(gal * unit_price, 2)})
shipments = pd.DataFrame(rows).sample(frac=1, random_state=0).reset_index(drop=True)
retailers = pd.DataFrame(retailers[["retailer_id", "retailer", "state", "lat", "lon"]])

print(f"{len(shipments):,} shipment lines, {shipments.retailer_id.nunique()} retailers, crop years "
      f"{shipments.crop_year.min()}-{shipments.crop_year.max()}")

2. Tag the brand and find the common product

Two derived columns do the pairing:

  • brand: PL if the line code starts with PL , else FB.
  • common: the line code with the brand prefix stripped, so PL SURFACTANT 90 and FB SURFACTANT 90 both become SURFACTANT 90.

Units are standardized before anything is summed. Quarts are converted to gallons first, so totals for one product don't mix units.

df = shipments.copy()
df["brand"] = np.where(df.line_code.str.startswith("PL "), "PL", "FB")
df["common"] = df.line_code.str.replace(r"^(PL|FB)\s+", "", regex=True)

quarts = df.uom.eq("QT")
df.loc[quarts, "qty"] = df.loc[quarts, "qty"] / 4
df.loc[quarts, "uom"] = "GA"

One row per retailer and common product, with flagship and private-label columns side by side (summed over 2021-2025). A product is a twin if anyone ever shipped a private-label version of it.

4. Private-label share, two ways

My first version averaged the per-product shares within each retailer. That gives a retailer's tiny private-label water conditioner order the same weight as its largest flagship surfactant line. The revenue-weighted share (total private-label revenue ÷ total twin revenue) is what actually answers "how much of this retailer's business is private label". The chart below shows both.

Also fixed: the old ratio of private label to flagship non-twin products divided by 1 whenever the denominator was 0, which silently turned the ratio into a raw dollar amount. It's now left blank.

tw = wide[wide.is_twin]
by_ret = tw.groupby("retailer_id").agg(rev_PL=("revenue_PL", "sum"), rev_FB=("revenue_FB", "sum"),
                                       qty_PL=("qty_PL", "sum"), qty_FB=("qty_FB", "sum"))
by_ret["pl_share_weighted"] = by_ret.rev_PL / (by_ret.rev_PL + by_ret.rev_FB)
by_ret["pl_share_mean"] = tw.assign(s=tw.revenue_PL / (tw.revenue_PL + tw.revenue_FB)) \
                            .groupby("retailer_id").s.mean()

non_twin = wide[~wide.is_twin].groupby("retailer_id").revenue_FB.sum().rename("rev_FB_exclusive")
by_ret = by_ret.join(non_twin)
by_ret["pl_vs_exclusive"] = by_ret.rev_PL / by_ret.rev_FB_exclusive.replace(0, np.nan)
by_ret["total_rev"] = by_ret[["rev_PL", "rev_FB", "rev_FB_exclusive"]].sum(axis=1)

by_ret = by_ret.join(retailers.set_index("retailer_id"))
big = by_ret[by_ret.total_rev > 250_000]
print(f"{len(big)} of {len(by_ret)} retailers have > $250k across the five years (smaller ones are too noisy)")
big.sort_values("pl_share_weighted", ascending=False)[

Takeaways

  • Pair through naming conventions. With no shared product key, prefix rules on the product-line code are enough to match twins.
  • Normalize units before you aggregate. Quarts and gallons have to agree before quantities are summed.
  • Weight by what you mean. An unweighted mean of shares answers a different question than revenue share. For this business question, revenue-weighted is the right one.
  • Guard ratios explicitly. Replacing a zero denominator with 1 hides the problem instead of flagging it.
